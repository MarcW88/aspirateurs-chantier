## Intent

The intent is clear and commercially defensible: compare vacuums primarily on wet/dry versatility, tank handling and practical cleanup rather than on dust-class certification alone.

## Preserve

- the clear wet/dry scope;
- the handoff to class/risk logic when hazardous dust becomes the real decision;
- current Bosch/Kärcher facts that remain verified;
- the distinction between motor power and useful suction.

## Blockers

1. **Candidate set is too Kärcher-heavy.** Two of the three products are WD models from the same family, which weakens an all-market comparison.
2. **The current score overweights raw airflow/depression.** Cross-brand manufacturer figures are not proven directly equivalent by measurement point/protocol.
3. **The page does not document why current professional wet/dry candidates or other brands were excluded.** A narrow shortlist is acceptable only if the screen is explicit.
4. **Legacy cloned structure.** The page is still rendered through the same ranking/method/model blocks as the other legacy comparisons.
5. **Wet/dry capability and dust safety are still too easy to conflate visually.** The rewrite should make the boundary explicit before product recommendations.

## Secondary improvements

- structure around use cases such as renovation cleanup, tool-connected workshop use, liquid recovery and larger-volume cleanup;
- compare tank/emptying/filter handling before raw suction figures;
- treat filter cleaning and tool-triggered start as workflow trade-offs, not universal quality scores.

## Missing data

- no persisted current market screen explaining why Nilfisk, Makita or other plausible wet/dry candidates were excluded;
- no GSC/SERP feature analysis was available in-repo for this audit.

## Next step

Keep the URL, rebuild the candidate universe, then write a scenario-led wet/dry comparison. The page should remain distinct from `/aspirateur-professionnel/` and `/aspirateur-chantier-puissant/` by making liquid handling and mixed-debris cleanup the primary decision.
